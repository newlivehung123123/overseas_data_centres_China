# Preliminary findings, 22 October 2026

This note sets out what the dataset of this repository shows so far about the data-centre capacity outside mainland China in which Chinese firms hold a role, and about the rules that cover the AI chips in that capacity. Every figure below comes from a row of the dataset, and every row cites the page that supports it and a passage that a script has found on that page.

## What the dataset holds

The dataset records 63 sites in 17 countries and territories, with 85 roles held by 13 firms, and 55 public cloud regions of Alibaba Cloud, Huawei Cloud, Tencent Cloud and BytePlus. It also records 10 reports of AI chips that Chinese firms reach outside China, 17 statements of capacity at sites, 8 statements of how much operators depend on Chinese customers, and 16 rules, bills, guidance notes, reviews, prosecutions and contracts that bear on those chips. The 274 rows carry 274 passages, and all 274 were found on their pages.

## Findings

**1. Chinese firms are reported to be the largest customers of three operators outside China.** DayOne, which began as the international arm of the Chinese operator GDS, states in its registration statement at the SEC that its largest customer, which it describes as a global technology company with a leading short-form video platform, provided 69.4 percent of its revenue in 2025 and 69.2 percent in the first half of 2026. Bloomberg reported in July 2025 that this customer is ByteDance. The British operator Nscale states in its own registration statement that its largest customer provided 73 percent of its revenue in 2025, and Tom's Hardware, citing the Financial Times, reports that this customer is a Singapore subsidiary of ByteDance. The Japanese operator Datasection received essentially all its AI infrastructure revenue in the year to March 2026, through one intermediary, from an end customer that it does not name, and the Financial Times has reported that this customer is Tencent.

**2. Chinese firms reach US chips outside China mainly by renting them.** In 9 of the 10 reports of AI chips, a Chinese firm rents the use of chips that another firm holds. The largest reported numbers of chips are about 100,000 advanced AI chips that Tencent leases at several Oracle data centres in Southeast Asia, as the Financial Times reported in October 2026, about 36,000 Nvidia B200 chips that ByteDance is set to reach through the cloud operator Aolani in Malaysia, and 2,304 Nvidia B200 chips that ByteDance's Singapore subsidiary rents at Nscale's data centre in Glomfjord, Norway. The last case rests on a loan agreement that Nscale filed at the SEC, which gives the number of chips and names the customer.

**3. The rules recorded so far control the shipment of the chips and not their use through a cloud service.** US export rules control exports and transfers of advanced chips, and access to the chips through a cloud service is not a violation of them. Guidance of May 2026 requires a licence for advanced chips destined for any entity whose ultimate parent is in China or Macau, wherever the entity is. The Remote Access Security Act, which would extend US export rules to access through a cloud service, passed the House by 369 votes to 22 on 12 January 2026 and has not passed the Senate. Malaysia has required a permit since July 2025 for exports, transshipments and transits of US-origin AI chips, and its trade ministry stated in June 2025 that servers with Nvidia and other AI chips are not controlled goods in Malaysia and that data centres may decide whom to serve within Malaysian law. The European Union extended its control list on exports to certain advanced computing integrated circuits in November 2025. The one control on remote use found so far is a clause of a private contract: the loan agreement for the chips at Glomfjord requires Nscale to monitor how its customer uses them for signs of a breach of export control law and to report such signs to the lender.

**4. Chinese ownership reaches beyond the firms usually counted as Chinese operators.** Global Switch, which owns and operates 12 completed data centres in Amsterdam, Frankfurt, Hong Kong, London, Madrid, Paris and Singapore and 4 under development, states in its annual report for 2025 that Jiangsu Shagang Group holds an indirect stake of 51.8 percent and controls the group. A subsidiary of Shagang holds a service agreement for 15 MW in the Hong Kong data centre until the end of 2027, and the report does not say who uses that capacity. GDS still holds 19.4 percent of DayOne.

**5. The capacity of one operator alone comes to 4,597 MW.** DayOne alone reports 962 MW in service, 1,328 MW under construction and 2,308 MW of secured powered land in 10 markets, or 4,597 MW in all, and Johor holds 789 MW of the 962 MW in service. Epoch AI estimates 1,210 MW of IT power at the 4 sites of this dataset that its database also holds. 19 of the 63 sites have an announced capacity of at least 20 MW or a report of AI chips, which places them at level B of the codebook.

## What the dataset adds to existing data

Epoch AI's database of AI data centres holds 95 data centres, 77 of them in the United States and 5 in mainland China, and 4 of the other 13 are sites in this dataset. Epoch estimates the IT power and the chips of each site from satellite imagery, but it does not record the role that each Chinese firm holds at a site or the rules that cover the site. This dataset records both, together with the ownership links and the dependence of operators on Chinese customers. A third layer, on government data centres financed by Chinese state lenders, is held back until an article on it has been published.

## Method

A person writes each row with the page that supports it and a passage copied from that page. A script downloads every page, including annual reports in PDF and filings at the SEC, and counts the passage as found when at least 90 percent of its overlapping pieces of eight characters are on the page. Every figure in this note is a value of a checked row or a count of checked rows, apart from the counts of Epoch's database, which come from Epoch's own file. The tests run on each push, and the dashboard at [newlivehung123123.github.io/overseas_data_centres_China](https://newlivehung123123.github.io/overseas_data_centres_China/) shows every row with its passage and the result of its check.

## Limits

The 63 sites are a first sample and not a census of any market. Several reports rest on unnamed sources cited by the Wall Street Journal, the Financial Times or Bloomberg, and the firms have not confirmed them, which the dataset records in the note of each row. Filings describe their largest customers without naming them, so the names come from press reports. The estimates of Epoch AI are models and are kept apart from the capacity that firms announce.

## Next steps

The next step is a census of Johor, the largest hub, that records every data centre in the state and the role of any Chinese firm in each. The study would then extend the census to the other hubs of Southeast Asia, the Gulf and Europe, add satellite imagery, permits and power allocations for the largest sites, record the rules of every host country, and publish the government data centre layer.
