# Changelog

This file lists the versions of the dataset and the changes between them. Each version carries a tag of the form `v0.3.1` on the commit at which the version was complete, and the tags of versions 0.1.0 to 0.3.0 were added on 23 November 2026 to the commits named below. `docs/outputs.csv` links each version to the publications that cite it, with the outlet, the status, the date, the link and the figures of each.

## Changes after version 0.3.1

1. A file, `data/remote_access_rule.csv`, written by `scripts/flag_remote_access.py`, that sorts each of the 91 sites by the way in which the US rule of 19 November 2026 on remote access can apply to the site. At 19 sites a firm headquartered or controlled in mainland China or Hong Kong is named as a tenant or user, at 39 further sites such a firm operates or owns the site, and at the other 33 sites the only firms named are former subsidiaries of Chinese firms, a fund of a Chinese state lender or a supplier of equipment. 8 of the 19 sites with a named customer are operational, and each of the 8 needed a licence by 17 February 2027 to continue if it provided more than 12,000,000 aggregate TPP to the customer. The file decides no licence, because the sources give neither the TPP of the chips at a site nor the share of the computing power that each customer uses.
2. A site, `MYS-0026`, for the data centre of 120 MW of IT capacity that Epoch Digital, a firm of the private equity firm Actis, is building in the Nusa Cemerlang Industrial Park in Johor. The Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank, which lends to the project, states that the first phase of 60 MW is fully contracted under an off-take agreement with BytePlus, so ByteDance holds a role as tenant on a primary source, and Bloomberg reports that Bank of China is one of the lead arrangers of the loan of US$488 million, a role graded unconfirmed because it rests on people whom the report does not name. The census of Johor had coded the facility, which Baxtel lists as Epoch Digital: Johor, as none found, and now codes it as tenant, so a role is recorded at 31 of the 84 facilities of Johor and at 74 of the 639 facilities of the seven markets. The low estimate of capacity under construction or planned rises from 6,353 MW to 6,473 MW and the high estimate from 7,464 MW to 7,584 MW.
3. A role of Bank of China as financier at DayOne's Nusajaya Tech Park, from the release of 8 December 2023 in which GDS names Bank of China (Malaysia) among the four lenders of the green loan of MYR1.27 billion for plot 1 of the site, and a row for Bank of China in `firms.csv` on the ground of state lender. The dataset now holds 91 sites and 118 roles of 23 firms and funds, of which 3 roles are financier.
4. Megaspeed's origin is now 7Road International, the Chinese gaming firm under whose name Megaspeed was incorporated in 2015 and which took the name Megaspeed in 2023, as Fortune reports, in place of an unnamed Chinese gaming firm from which an earlier source said Megaspeed was spun off. The relation in `firm_links.csv` is the new value former name.
5. A log of the 12 web searches of 23 November 2026 for loans and investments of Chinese state lenders and Chinese banks, in `config/finance_searches.csv`. The loan of US$490 million to Chindata for a facility in Malaysia, among whose lenders a report names China Merchants Bank, China Minsheng Bank and China CITIC Bank International, is not yet coded, because the pages that list the lenders refuse automated downloads, so their passages cannot be checked.
6. A table of loans and investments, `data/financing.csv`, with one row for each loan or stake of a Chinese lender or fund, the borrower, the amount and the currency as the source gives them, the role of the lender in the deal and, where the source names one, the site. A row tied to a site must match a role of the lender as financier at that site in `site_roles.csv`, which `scripts/check_sources.py` checks. The table holds 4 rows: the two loans of Bank of China above, the loan for Bridge Data Centres to which Industrial Bank, a bank of Fuzhou, contributed US$142 million as lead arranger and bookrunner, which its release ties to no site, and the investment of the China-ASEAN Investment Cooperation Fund II in Racks Central. Industrial Bank has a row in `firms.csv` on the ground of headquarters, from the registered address in its annual report.
7. A file, `docs/outputs.csv`, that links each version to the publications that cite it: version 0.1.0 to the public post of 9 November 2026 on AI in Society and the EA Forum, and version 0.3.1 to the cover story submitted to The Wire China in November 2026, whose title and link are added on publication.
8. An option `--only-new` for `scripts/check_sources.py`, which keeps the result already written for every row whose page and passage are unchanged and downloads only the pages of new or changed rows.
9. Censuses of the three markets of Brazil on Baxtel, of 23 November 2026: 113 facilities in Greater São Paulo, 20 in Rio de Janeiro and 10 in Fortaleza, in `config/sao_paulo_census.csv`, `config/rio_de_janeiro_census.csv` and `config/fortaleza_census.csv`, with 9 web searches that raise the log of `config/census_searches.csv` to 118. A role is recorded at 2 of the 143 facilities, both the data centre at Pecém, which Baxtel lists under ByteDance and under Omnia, and no source found names a Chinese firm at the other 141. The censuses now cover 782 facilities in ten markets, and a role is recorded at 76 of them. A new script, `scripts/list_census_facilities.py`, writes the list of a market from Baxtel's page, and `scripts/scan_census_pages.py` gains an option `--only-new` that scans only the pages of facilities that `data/census_page_scan.csv` does not hold yet.
10. A role of the China-LAC Industrial Cooperation Investment Fund as financier at the data centre at Pecém, from The Brazilian Report, which states that the Chinese state fund is acquiring an indirect stake in the facility through Omnia DC Holding I, a row for the fund in `firms.csv` on the ground of state lender fund, a link to China Development Bank as sponsor in `firm_links.csv`, and the stake as `FIN-0005` in `financing.csv`. The dataset now holds 119 roles of 24 firms and funds, of which 4 are financier.

## Version 0.3.1, 23 November 2026

Version 0.3.1 carries the tag `v0.3.1` and is the version that the cover story submitted to The Wire China in November 2026 cites, so the figures, notes and counts of the story can be checked against the files of this tag. The version holds 1,044 graded rows, of which 223 rest on a primary source, 799 on a secondary source and 22 are unconfirmed. The passages of the 2 new rows were found on their page. The sites, roles, censuses and capacity estimate are those of version 0.3.0. Version 0.3.1 makes the following changes to version 0.3.0.

1. Two rows for the interim final rule "Addressing Remote Access to Advanced Semiconductors" (91 FR 74970), which the US Department of Commerce published on 19 November 2026 and which took effect on publication: `USA-0013` records the licence requirement for exports of advanced chips to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil, and `USA-0014` records the licence requirement for chips used at data centres that provide more than 12,000,000 aggregate TPP to firms headquartered, or whose ultimate parent company is headquartered, in Country Group D:5 or Macau. Both rows cite the official PDF on GovInfo, because FederalRegister.gov refuses automated downloads. The notes of `USA-0001` and `USA-0010` now refer to the rule, and the rules table holds 22 rows, of which 8 rest on a primary source.
2. Three interactive figures for the article on Racks Central's data centres in Johor, published on GitHub Pages from `site/figures/`: `financing-chain.html`, which traces the capital from the sponsors of CAF II to Racks Central's sites in Johor and Batam, `map.html`, which maps the 90 sites and opens on the world or on Johor, Singapore and Batam with `?view=world` or `?view=strait`, and `seven-markets.html`, which charts the share of the facilities in the seven censuses at which a Chinese firm or fund holds a role. `site/figures/img/` holds an image of each figure for print.

### Corrections

1. Four facilities of the census of Johor carried names that Baxtel invents for facilities whose names it withholds, such as "Hub Max" and "Station Gamma". The names are now empty, and the passages start at the words that Baxtel shows in place of the name.
2. The capacity estimate counted the whole planned total of a site beside the capacity already in service at the same site. Each estimate now counts only the part of a planned total that exceeds the capacity in service, so the 150 MW planned at Hamar adds 60 MW to the 90 MW in service.
3. The capacity estimate now leaves out sites at which a Chinese firm is only the builder or the supplier of equipment, because the codebook counts the capacity of a site only where a Chinese firm owns, operates, rents, uses or finances the site.
4. The row on the land of Megaspeed in Johor now records that the purchase was completed in September 2026.
5. Version 0.1.0 left Bridge Data Centres out because Bridge has never had a controlling owner in mainland China, a test that would also have left out DayOne. Under the standard of version 0.2.0, Bridge counts by its origin in ChinData Group, which had its headquarters in Beijing, and 23 facilities of Bridge in the censuses of Johor, Greater Kuala Lumpur and Greater Bangkok are recoded as operator. The 4 buildings of MY06, where ByteDance is the anchor tenant, are now coded by their operator, because the census codes the first of operator, owner, tenant and financier.

### Effect on the capacity estimate

The low estimate of capacity in service rose from 1,323 MW in version 0.1.0 to 1,416 MW, and the high estimate rose from 1,652 MW to 1,654 MW. The capacity under construction or planned rose from between 4,183 MW and 5,041 MW to between 5,052 MW and 6,006 MW, mainly because of the data centres in Rayong that Thailand's Board of Investment approved, Bitdeer AI's site in Cyberjaya and the two data centres of NexAI in Indonesia. Without the 4 firms that count as Chinese on their origin alone, version 0.2.0 gives between 452 MW and 585 MW in service and between 1,259 MW and 1,917 MW under construction or planned. The public post of November 2026, "Where Chinese AI firms rent the chips they cannot buy", gives the figures of version 0.1.0.
