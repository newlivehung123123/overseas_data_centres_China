Note: MW denotes megawatts. Arrows show the direction of capital. ESR Group gives the capacity of RCJM1 as 90 MW [2]; the figure uses the 70 MW of IT load given by Racks Central [5]. Neither Racks Central nor ESR Group has disclosed the size of the stake of CAF II or the customers of the Johor complex, and Racks Central has not named the financing partners who attended the ceremony of 29 June 2026 [2, 5]. Since 19 November 2026, a US licence has been required to export advanced chips to Malaysia, unless the ultimate consignee or end user is headquartered in the United States, or has an ultimate parent company headquartered there, and the exporter files monthly reports. A licence is also required to export such chips to a data centre outside the United States that supplies significant volumes of computing power to companies headquartered in Macau or in a country under a US arms embargo (Country Group D:5), which includes China [9].
